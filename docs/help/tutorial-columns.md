# Tutorial: columns that turn

A satin column is the shiny stitch of embroidery: each stitch goes from
one side of the column to the other. In this tutorial you make columns
in three more ways, and you see where each one is the best tool. You
also make a satin from a vector line.

## Open the start design

Click [Open the start design](sample:columns). The picture shows an
arch, an S and a crescent. The wave at the bottom right is a vector
line: a shape with no stitches.

![The shapes to sew as columns](columns-start)

## The arch: Column Pairs

With **Column Pairs** you click the two sides at the same time, one pair
after the other. Each pair also sets the angle of the stitches there.
This is good for a column that turns or changes its width.

1. Click **Digitize > Columns > Column Pairs**.
2. Click a point on the outer side of the arch, then the point across
   from it on the inner side. Do this at the left end, at three places
   on the arch, and at the right end.
3. Push **Enter**.

## The S: Column C

With **Column C** you click only the middle line. The column has the
same width (3 mm) all along. This is good for thin lines of the same
width, for example the strokes of a hand-drawn letter.

1. Click **Digitize > Columns > Column C**.
2. Click along the middle of the S: a left click at each end, right
   clicks on the curves.
3. Push **Enter**.

## The crescent: Turning

A **Turning** satin is an outline with angle lines. The stitches turn
from one angle line to the next. This is good for shapes such as petals
and leaves that are too wide for one column.

1. Click **Digitize > Fills > Turning**.
2. Click round the crescent: a left click at each point, right clicks
   on the two curves. Push **Enter**.
3. Click two points for each angle line, across the crescent: one line
   near the left end, one in the middle, one near the right end.
4. Push **Enter**, then push **Esc** two times.

A turning satin sews in sections, so its stitches are not too long. The
setting **Split stitches over** in Object Properties sets how long a
stitch can be.

## The wave: from a vector line

1. Click **Digitize > Select > Select**, and click the wave.
2. Click **Artwork > Convert > To Satin**. The line becomes a satin
   column of the default width.
3. Push **Esc**.

## Look at the stitches

Click **View > Show > Thread Look**, and select **Thread Look** in its
menu. Look at the angle of the stitches: they turn with each column.

![The four columns with the thread look](columns-stitches)

## Check it

- The status line shows **Objects: 4** and no warning.
- Select a column, then click **Digitize > Select > Reshape**: you can
  move the points of its sides, and its pairs.
- A satin wider than about 10 mm has long, loose stitches. Use a
  turning satin with angle lines, or a fill, for a wider shape.

## If it does not look right

- **The stitches of the arch cross each other.** A pair was not across
  from its point: click **Undo**, and click each pair straight across
  the column.
- **The crescent has no stitches.** It needs at least two angle lines
  that go across it. Click **Undo**, and draw it again.
- **To Satin is gray.** The selected object is not a vector. Select the
  wave line.
