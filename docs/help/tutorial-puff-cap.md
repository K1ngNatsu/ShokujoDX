# Tutorial: puff on a cap

In this tutorial you make the letters of a cap front sew over foam (3D
puff), put the design on a cap frame, and sew it in the cap order.

A cap frame is for multi-needle machines with a cap driver. The cap
turns under the needle, so a design on a cap sews from the middle out
and from the bottom up.

## Open the start design

Click [Open the start design](sample:cap-front). It has a navy frame (a
running line) and the red lettering **CAP** (Block Sans, 20 mm).

## The machine, the cap frame and the fabric

1. Click the **Design** tab. In **Machine**, select **Tajima
   multi-needle** (or your machine).
2. In **Hoop**, select **Wide cap frame 360 x 75 (270 degree class)**.
3. Click **Design > Fabric and Styles > Fabric**. In the **Fabric** list,
   select **Structured cap**, and click **Apply**. A cap is stiff and
   curved: the fabric setting adds underlay and pull compensation.

## Puff letters

1. Drag a box round the lettering to select it (not the frame).
2. Click **Specialty > Puff and Laydown > Puff**.

The letters sew over foam now:

- The satin is dense, and the underlay has no rows across the columns:
  they would press the foam flat.
- Stitches go over each end of a column, so the needle cuts the foam and
  the end is closed.
- The machine stops before the first puff object, so you can put the
  foam on. The worksheet says so. Tear the foam away after the puff
  objects.

For a satin column, the box **Puff (3D foam)** in Object Properties does
the same. Use columns 3 mm wide or more, and foam of a color like the
thread.

Design Info now shows "Thread builds up" at the point of the **A**: the
two legs of the letter lie on each other there, and puff satin is dense.
A letter with sharp points has more thread at its points than a letter
with blunt ends. For much puff text, use a heavy font with blunt ends,
and look at the points in **View > Show > Density Map**.

## The cap order

1. Click **Arrange > Sew Order > Cap Order**. Each color sews from the
   middle out and from the bottom up, and the lettering sews from its
   middle letter out. The colors and what is on top stay as they are.
2. Click **View > Show > Thread Look**, and select **Thread Look** in its
   menu.

![Puff letters on a cap front](puff-cap-stitches)

Design Info checks the order: on a cap frame, it says when the sew order
is not from the middle out.

## If it does not look right

- **The cap frame is not in the list.** The machine has no cap driver.
  Select a multi-needle machine.
- **Puff is gray.** Select satin columns or lettering first.
- **The foam shows at the ends of the columns.** Use wider columns, and
  thicker thread or more density.
