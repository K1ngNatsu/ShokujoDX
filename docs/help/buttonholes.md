# Buttonholes and eyelets

The embroidery machine can sew buttonholes and eyelets with the design, so
you do not change to a sewing machine. **Digitize > Buttonholes** opens the
Buttonholes window.

## The shapes

| Shape | Use |
|---|---|
| Square ends | The usual buttonhole: shirts, blouses and most woven fabrics. |
| Round end | A round end for the shank of the button: blouses, dresses and trousers. |
| Round ends | Soft round ends: light, fine and sheer fabrics. |
| Keyhole | An eye for a thick button with a shank: coats, jackets and heavy fabrics. |
| Tapered keyhole | A keyhole with a tapered end: tailored jackets and coats. |
| Stretch (for knits) | Open zigzag legs that stretch with the fabric: knits and jersey. |
| Eyelet | A round hole for a cord, a lace, a belt or a drawstring. |

Each buttonhole sews a running box first (it holds the fabric), then the
satin legs with a gap between them for the cut, then the ends. The picture
in the window shows the shape.

## The size

- **Button** > **Standard sizes**: choose the diameter of your button, in
  inches or in mm. The size in lignes (L), the unit of button makers, is in
  brackets: 20L is 1/2 in.
- **Button thickness**: 2 mm is a usual flat button. A domed button or a
  button with a shank needs more.
- **Opening**: the length of the cut, the diameter of the button and its
  thickness. Type a length to set it yourself, for example `5/8 in`.
- For an eyelet, **Hole** is the diameter of the hole.
- **Scale the stitches with the size**: the legs, the gap, the bar tacks
  and the eye follow the size, so a small buttonhole is narrow and a large
  one is wide. Turn it off to keep your own settings.
- **Stitches**: the **Leg width**, the **Gap for the cut**, the **Bar
  tack**, the **Eye** of a keyhole and the **Density**.
- **Corded**: a run under each leg, as a cord. The buttonhole is stronger
  and stands up more.
- **Mitre corners** (square ends, round end and keyhole): the legs and the
  bar tacks sew as one column that turns at the corners, with no stops.
  Bar tacks hold the ends of the cut better: use mitre corners on light
  fabrics and for decoration.
- **Turn end for end**: the round end, the eye or the taper goes to the
  other end. Put it at the side of the button shank: near the edge of the
  garment for a level buttonhole.

## Place buttonholes

- With no line selected, **Place one buttonhole** puts one in the middle
  of the hoop, **Upright** or **Level**.
- Draw a line where the buttonholes go (Digitize > Line), select it, then
  click **Place buttonholes**. The line is used up. Space the buttonholes
  by the **Number** (the first and the last at the ends of the line) or by
  the **Distance apart** (from the start of the line). **Across the line**
  turns them across the line, for example on a coat; else they go along
  it, for example on a shirt placket.

The buttonholes of one click are one group. A string that is larger than
the hoop sews in hoopings: see [Designs larger than the hoop](hooping).

## Change buttonholes

Select buttonholes that you placed, then click **Digitize > Buttonholes**:
the window shows their settings. Change the settings and click **Change
the selected buttonholes**. Each buttonhole is made again in its place,
also after you moved, turned or mirrored it. Undo takes back the change in
one step. A buttonhole whose box you deleted is not made again.

## Sew and cut

1. Hoop the fabric with a backing. On a light fabric, put interfacing
   under the buttonholes. On knits, use a cut-away backing and the
   **Stretch (for knits)** shape.
2. Sew the design. The machine sews the buttonholes as the other objects.
3. Put a pin across each end, then cut the opening between the legs with a
   seam ripper or a buttonhole cutter. Cut or punch the hole of an eyelet
   and the eye of a keyhole.
4. Test the size on a scrap of the same fabric first: the button must go
   through the opening easily.
