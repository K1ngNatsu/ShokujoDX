# Plan: roadmap phase P7 (hooping, layouts and presentation)

Date: 2026-10-23 · Status: Accepted. Milestones 1 to 6 done in software; the exit test (kit in `out/p7-sewout`, made again for the upright hoop, ADR 0034) is open. Audit: `docs/design/roadmap.md`, "Audit of P7".

## Goal

A design of any size sews on the hoops that the user has, and the user
can show the design on a product before it sews (roadmap P7). The exit
test: a 360 × 260 mm design, split for the 260 × 200 mm hoop of the
Topaz 20, sews with aligned joins.

The exit tests of P2 and P3 (users), P5 and P6 (sew-outs, kits in
`out/p5-sewout` and `out/p6-sewout`) are still open; the P1 sew-out is
covered by the later kits (owner decision). The owner started this phase
on 2026-10-23.

Sources for what each tool does: the public documentation of commercial
embroidery products and common practice, in our own words (clean-room
rule).

## Order

Each milestone ends with something a user can try in the app.

| # | Scope | Exit test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Hoop placement.** Turn the hoop by an angle (±15° steps or a number); the stitches turn back when they go to the machine, so the hoop is straight on the machine. Hoop placement: manual, or auto centering (the hoop center follows the center of the design). Auto hoop: the smallest hoop that the design fits (from My hoops, else the hoops of the machine), also with the design turned 90°. | 2 | **Multi-hooping engine.** A layout of hoopings on a large design (ADR 0032): the hoop, the hooping centers and angles (0° or 90°), split lines, an overlap, registration marks. Auto layout: the fewest hoopings of the hoop that cover the design, with the overlap. The split lines cut the plane into zones; each zone goes to a hooping that it fits. Objects with an area (fills, appliqué, vectors with a fill) and lines are cut at the split lines, with the overlap; objects that cannot be cut (satin columns, lettering) go whole to the hooping that has most of them, with a warning if they do not fit. Registration marks (small crosses) sew at the end of a hooping and again at the start of the next, in a thread of their own, so the machine stops and the user can check the alignment. Each hooping is a design of its own, made from the layout when it is needed (not kept). | Corpus design `multi-hoop` (360 × 260 mm on the 260 × 200 hoop) with a golden snapshot of each hooping; unit tests on cuts and on the overlap. |
| 3 | **Hooping mode in the app.** A Hooping tab: hooping mode on and off; the hoopings on the canvas with their numbers; drag, turn, add and delete a hooping; draw, move and delete split lines; Auto layout; options (hoop, overlap, margin, marks). Preview hoopings (a picture of each hooping, with its objects). Output: one machine file for each hooping (`name_1.vp3`, `name_2.vp3`, …), and a worksheet with the order of the hoopings and where each one goes. | The exit test kit in `out/p7-sewout`: the 360 × 260 design in four VP3 files and the worksheet. |
| 4 | **Multi-position hoops.** A hoop preset can have fixed positions (each with a field and its offset from the hoop center). The layout of such a hoop comes from its positions; the output is one file for each position, as for multi-hooping. | Unit tests; corpus design with a test hoop of two positions. |
| 5 | **Product backdrops and virtual decoration.** Products drawn in code (T-shirt, polo, sweatshirt, cap, bag, towel, cushion) in a color that the user chooses, at real size, with the common places for a design (left chest, center chest, back, sleeve, cap front). A picture of the user's own product, with its real width. The design shows on the product (with the thread look); the product does not sew. Save a picture of the design on the product (virtual decoration image). | A design on each product; the saved picture. |
| 6 | **Design repeats.** Show the design again in a row or a grid (spacing, count), to check the join of a continuous design (border strips) or of an overlapping design. Make repeats: copies of the design as objects. With auto start and end at the two ends of the design, the needle goes from one copy to the next. | Corpus design `repeats` with a golden snapshot. |

Then the exit test (roadmap P7 exit): the 360 × 260 mm design, sewn in
four hoopings on the Topaz 20, with photos of the joins.

## Decisions

- **The hoop angle is an output transform (ADR 0032).** The objects do not
  move or turn when the hoop turns; the canvas shows the hoop turned, and
  the stitch plan turns the stitches back, so the machine file has the
  hoop straight. The quarter turns of the design (`Design::rotation`) stay
  as they are. `Design::output_transform` is the one function from design
  coordinates to machine coordinates.
- **Hoopings are made, not kept (ADR 0032).** The design keeps the layout
  (hoop, centers, split lines, overlap, marks). The objects of each
  hooping come from the layout each time, so an edit of the design
  changes all hoopings, and there is no copy to keep up to date.
- **Products are drawn in code.** No garment photos that we did not make;
  the user can add a picture of their own product.
